Forest Type Rainfall & Temp Key Characteristics & Adaptations Important Trees Major Distribution (Regions/States)
1. Tropical Evergreen (Rainforests) Rain: > 200 cm
Temp: 25°C–27°C
Humidity: > 77%		 • Evergreen: Trees have different shedding times, so forest never looks bare.
• Multi-layered canopy (40–60m tall) blocks sunlight; dark, damp floor.
• Mixed species (not in pure stands) → hard to exploit commercially.		 Rosewood, Ebony, Mahogany, Cinchona, Rubber, Gurjan, Toon, Shisham Windward Western Slope of Western Ghats (Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra), Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Garo-Khasi-Jaintia Hills (Meghalaya), Assam.
2. Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon Forests)
(Moist: 100–200 cm)
(Dry: 70–100 cm)		 Rain: 70 to 200 cm
Temp: 24°C–27°C		 • Shed leaves for 6–8 weeks in dry summer (March–May) to conserve water/check transpiration.
• Pure stands: Trees occur in single-species clusters.
• Most commercially exploited & most widespread forest in India.		 Teak, Sal, Sandalwood, Semul, Myrobalan, Palas, Mahua, Arjun, Mulberry Leeward Eastern Slope of Western Ghats, Foothills of Himalayas (Siwaliks), Chota Nagpur Plateau, Odisha, MP, Chhattisgarh, UP, Bihar, Karnataka.
3. Tropical Desert (Thorn & Scrub) Rain: < 50 cm (often < 25 cm)
Temp: 25°C–27°C (Low humidity < 47%)		 • Xerophytic adaptations to survive acute drought:
1. Long taproots to draw deep underground water.
2. Leaves modified into sharp spines/thorns to reduce transpiration.
3. Fleshy stems / thick waxy bark to store water & prevent grazing.		 Babool (Kikar), Ber, Date Palm, Khair, Neem, Cacti, Khejri Western Rajasthan (Thar Desert), Kutch & Saurashtra (Gujarat), South-West Punjab, Haryana, Rain-shadow belt of Deccan Plateau.
4. Littoral (Tidal / Mangrove / Deltaic) Rain: 150–200 cm (Wet coastal deltas)
Temp: 26°C–29°C		 • Grow in saline, waterlogged, marshy deltaic soil washed by tides:
1. Breathing Roots (Pneumatophores): Vertical roots with pores protruding above mud to take in oxygen during low tide.
2. Stilt / Prop Roots: Tangled root network above ground supporting trunk in soft shifting mud during high tide.		 Sundari, Gorjan, Hintal, Keora, Rhizophora, Agar, Casuarina Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta (Sundarbans in West Bengal), Deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, and Andaman Coast.
5. Mountain (Montane) Forests Rain: 100–250 cm
Temp: 12°C–20°C (Varies with Altitude)		 • Altitudinal Zonation: Vegetation changes from Tropical Deciduous (foothills) → Temperate Broad-leafed (1000–2000m) → Coniferous (1500–3300m) → Alpine Grass/Moss (>3300m).
• Conifers have conical shape & needle-like leaves to slide off snow.		 Chir Pine, Deodar, Silver Fir, Blue Pine, Spruce, Oak, Chestnut, Magnolia, Birch
(Nilgiris: Sholas / Eucalyptus)		 Himalayan Region (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh) and Nilgiri / Anamalai / Palani Hills in South India.
Tree Name Forest Type & Key State Official CISCE Board Economic Uses & Special Features
1. Teak Tropical Moist Deciduous (MP, Maharashtra, Assam) Moderately hard, durable, easy to season; contains natural oil that prevents rusting of iron nails. Used for ship-building, house construction, railway sleepers, and high-quality furniture.
2. Sal Tropical Moist Deciduous (Bihar, Odisha, Jharkhand, UP) Very hard, heavy, tough, and immune to termite attack. Used for making railway sleepers, bridges, beams, door frames, and mine props.
3. Sandalwood Tropical Deciduous (Karnataka — #1 State, Tamil Nadu) Aromatic heartwood; used for extracting sandalwood oil for perfumery, cosmetics, soaps, incense sticks (agarbattis), and carving ornamental handicrafts/statues.
4. Sundari Littoral / Tidal / Mangrove (Sundarbans Delta, West Bengal) Provides very hard, strong, durable, and water-resistant timber that does not rot in salty water. Used for making boats, barges, telephone posts, and house construction.
5. Rosewood Tropical Evergreen (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) Fine-grained, extremely hard, dark reddish-brown wood. Used for expensive luxury furniture, musical instruments, floorings, veneers, and ornamental carvings.
6. Ebony Tropical Evergreen (Andaman, Western Ghats) Coal-black, heavy, hard lustrous wood. Used for piano keys, ornamental carvings, musical instruments, and inlaid decorative furniture.
7. Semul (Silk Cotton) Tropical Moist Deciduous (Assam, Bihar, Tamil Nadu) Soft and white timber. Used for making matchboxes, matchsticks, toys, packing cases, and plywood; its fruit yields soft fibre for stuffing pillows.
8. Myrobalan (Harar) Tropical Deciduous (MP, Odisha, Maharashtra) Its fruit/nuts provide rich tannin used for tanning leather and for dyeing cotton, wool, and silk, as well as Ayurvedic medicines.
9. Palas (Flame of Forest) Tropical Dry Deciduous (Chota Nagpur, Bihar, MP) Leaves are used for rearing shellac worms (lac cultivation); flowers yield natural dye.
10. Babool (Kikar) Tropical Desert / Thorn (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab) Bark and pods are used for tanning leather; yields valuable gum (Gum Arabic); thorny branches used for fencing and medicinal twigs (datun).
11. Neem Tropical Desert / Dry Deciduous (All across Plains) Bark, leaves, and seeds have powerful medicinal, antibacterial, and antifungal properties; used in cosmetics, soaps, medicines, and bio-pesticides/insecticides.
12. Khair (Acacia Catechu) Tropical Desert / Thorn (Rajasthan, UP, MP) Boiled heartwood yields ‘Kattha’ (used in paan / betel leaf and medicines) and Cutch (used for dyeing and tanning).
13. Deodar (Devadaru) Mountain / Coniferous (Himachal Pradesh, Kashmir) Medium-weight, oily, durable, scented wood resistant to rot. Used for railway sleepers, bridge construction, beams, and window frames.
14. Chir Pine & Silver Fir Mountain / Coniferous (Uttarakhand, HP, J&K) Chir Pine yields resin and turpentine oil (used in paints/varnishes) and wood for tea chests. Silver Fir soft wood is used for paper pulp, matchsticks, and packing cases.
