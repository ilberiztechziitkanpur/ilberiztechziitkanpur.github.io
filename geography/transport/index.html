Transport Mode Best Suited For (Cargo & Terrain) Top 3 Advantages (Pros) Top 3 Disadvantages (Cons) Governing Body & Key Highlights
1. Roadways • Short & medium distances.
• Perishable goods (milk, fruits, vegetables, flowers).
• Steep hilly/mountainous terrain.		 • Provides Door-to-Door service (reduces loading/unloading & pilferage cost).
• Can be built on steep gradients & sharp curves in hills.
• Acts as a feeder connecting railway stations, airfields, and sea ports.		 • Unmetalled (Kuchcha) roads become muddy and unusable during monsoons.
• Not economical for bulky goods (coal/iron ore) over long distances.
• High traffic congestion, road accidents, vehicle pollution & multiple toll check-posts.		 • NHAI (National Highways Authority of India — 1995).
• BRO (Border Roads Organisation — 1960).
• PMGSY (Rural roads).
2. Railways
("Principal Mode / Backbone")		 • Long-distance overland travel.
• Heavy & bulky raw materials (coal, iron ore, steel, cement, food grains, fertilizers).
• Flat, level plains.		 • Most dependable & economical land mode for transporting bulky goods over long distances.
• Comfortable, safe, high-capacity passenger travel.
• Promotes national integration & rapid troop movement for defense.		 • Lacks door-to-door service (requires road transport at both ends, increasing transshipment cost).
• Cannot negotiate steep mountain slopes, sandy deserts, or dense marshy forests easily.
• Gauge differences & high capital/maintenance cost.		 • Ministry of Railways (18+ Zones).
• 3 Gauges: Broad ($1.676\text{ m}$), Metre ($1.0\text{ m}$), Narrow ($0.762\text{ m}$ / $0.610\text{ m}$).
• Konkan Railway (Roha to Thokur/Mangalore).
3. Waterways
(Inland + Oceanic/Coastal)		 • Heaviest & bulkiest non-perishable cargo (crude oil, iron ore, machinery, timber).
• International foreign trade (~95% by volume).		 • Cheapest mode of transport (no cost of constructing or maintaining tracks/roads).
• Most fuel-efficient and eco-friendly (least carbon emissions per tonne-km).
• Huge carrying capacity for heavy cargo.		 • Slowest mode of transport (unsuitable for urgent or perishable cargo).
• Inland rivers suffer from seasonal drying in summer, silting, and waterfalls (Peninsular rivers).
• Limited reach (only where navigable rivers/coasts exist) & weather/cyclone risks.		 • IWAI (Inland Waterways Authority of India — 1986).
• NW-1 (Ganga), NW-2 (Brahmaputra), NW-3 (Kerala).
• 12 Major Sea Ports & ~200 Minor Ports.
4. Airways • Very long distances where time is critical.
• Light, costly, and perishable goods (gems, medicines, electronics, mail).
• Inaccessible hills (North-East/Ladakh) & disaster relief.		 • Fastest and most comfortable mode of transport.
• Free from physical/relief barriers (flies effortlessly over lofty mountains, oceans, deserts, and dense forests).
• Indispensable during natural calamities (floods, earthquakes) & defense evacuations.		 • Most expensive (costliest) mode of transport (out of reach of common people).
• Very limited carrying capacity (cannot carry bulky freight like coal or iron ore).
• Highly vulnerable to bad weather (dense fog, storms) & uses costly Aviation Turbine Fuel (ATF).		 • AAI (Airports Authority of India — 1995) & DGCA.
• Pawan Hans Helicopters Ltd. (ONGC offshore oil & hilly North-East).
• UDAN Scheme (Regional air connectivity).
Category / Project Exact Route / Location / Terminal Points Official CISCE Board Keywords & Special Significance
1. Golden Quadrilateral (GQ) Connects 4 Metro Cities: Delhi – Mumbai – Chennai – Kolkata ($5,846\text{ km}$, 4/6-lane super-highways managed by NHAI). Connects major industrial, agricultural, and cultural centres to major ports; reduces travel time, fuel consumption, and vehicle wear-and-tear.
2. North–South & East–West Corridors • N–S Corridor: Srinagar (J&K) to Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu) ($4,000\text{ km}$).
• E–W Corridor: Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat) ($3,300\text{ km}$).
• Meeting Junction: Jhansi (Uttar Pradesh).		 Forms the backbone of the National Highways Development Project (NHDP), integrating the extreme corners of India.
3. Border Roads Organisation (BRO) Set up in May 1960; operates along Northern & North-Eastern borders (Ladakh, HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal). Built the world’s highest motorable roads (Umling La / Khardung La) & Atal Tunnel; vital for rapid movement of defense troops/supplies, snow clearance, and economic development of remote border hills.
4. National Waterway 1 (NW-1) Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly River System: From Prayagraj (Allahabad) in UP to Haldia in West Bengal ($1,620\text{ km}$). Longest National Waterway in India; passes through UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, and West Bengal.
5. National Waterway 2 (NW-2) & NW-3 • NW-2 (Brahmaputra River): From Sadiya to Dhubri in Assam ($891\text{ km}$).
• NW-3 (West Coast Canal): Kollam to Kottapuram in Kerala ($205\text{ km}$, with Champakara & Udyogmandal canals).		 NW-2 connects North-East India and transports tea, jute, coal, and petroleum. NW-3 is India’s first waterway with 24-hour navigation facilities.
6. West Coast Major Sea Ports (6 Ports)
(Mostly Natural Harbours)		 1. Kandla (Deendayal Port) — Gujarat
2. Mumbai — Maharashtra
3. Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPT / Nhava Sheva) — Navi Mumbai
4. Mormugao — Goa
5. New Mangalore — Karnataka
6. Kochi (Cochin) — Kerala		 • Kandla: Tidal Port & Free Trade Zone at Kutch; built after Partition to replace Karachi port.
• Mumbai: Biggest natural harbour; handles maximum traffic.
• Nhava Sheva (JNPT): Largest ultra-modern containerized port; built to decongest Mumbai port.
• Mormugao & New Mangalore: Famous for exporting Iron Ore (Kudremukh iron ore via Mangalore).
• Kochi: Natural lagoon harbour ("Queen of the Arabian Sea").
7. East Coast Major Sea Ports (6 Ports) 1. Tuticorin (V.O. Chidambaranar) — Tamil Nadu
2. Chennai — Tamil Nadu
3. Ennore (Kamarajar Port) — Tamil Nadu
4. Visakhapatnam (Vizag) — Andhra Pradesh
5. Paradip — Odisha
6. Kolkata (with Haldia) — West Bengal		 • Chennai: Oldest artificial harbour on the East Coast (2nd largest by volume).
• Ennore: India’s first corporate port; built to decongest Chennai.
• Visakhapatnam: Deepest landlocked and best-protected natural port (protected by Dolphin’s Nose headland).
• Paradip: Deep-water port exporting Odisha iron ore & coal.
• Kolkata: Only Riverine Port (on Hooghly river, 128 km inland); suffers from heavy silting (needs constant dredging) & tidal bores. Haldia was built downstream to relieve pressure on Kolkata.
