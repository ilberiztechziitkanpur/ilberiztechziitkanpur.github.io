Method / Concept Target Waste & Mechanism Top 3 Advantages (Pros) Precautions / Limitations (Cons)
1. Segregation at Source
(First & Most Crucial Step)		 Separating waste at household/source level into:
• Green Bin: Wet / Biodegradable.
• Blue Bin: Dry / Recyclable.
• Red/Black Bin: Hazardous / Biomedical.		 • Prevents wet waste from contaminating dry recyclables.
• Makes composting of wet waste & recycling of dry waste easy.
• Drastically reduces the volume of waste sent to landfills & protects ragpickers from injury/infection.		 • Requires strict public awareness and civic discipline.
• Municipal trucks must have separate compartments so segregated waste is not mixed again.
2. Sanitary Landfill
(Engineered vs Open Dumping)		 • Waste is compacted in thin layers in a pit lined with impervious clay & plastic (HDPE) liners.
• Covered daily with a $15\text{ cm}$ layer of soil/earth.		 • Prevents breeding of vectors (flies, mosquitoes, rats) & foul odour due to daily soil cover.
• Bottom liner prevents ‘Leachate’ from polluting underground water.
• Reclaimed landfill top can be developed into parks, golf courses, or gardens; generates Methane ($CH_4$) for energy.		 • Must be located far away from human settlements, airports, and water bodies.
• Requires large land area and proper methane gas venting pipes to prevent underground fires/explosions.
3. Composting & Vermicomposting • Target: Wet biodegradable organic waste (kitchen peels, crop residue, leaves, cow dung).
• Vermicomposting: Uses earthworms (Red Wigglers) to decompose organic matter.		 • Converts foul organic waste into nutrient-rich natural humus/manure (rich in NPK).
• Enhances soil moisture-retention & reduces need for chemical fertilizers.
• Diverts ~50% of municipal waste away from landfills.		 • Cannot treat non-biodegradable waste (plastics, glass, metals, toxic chemicals).
• Vermicomposting requires shade, moisture, and moderate temperature so earthworms survive.
4. Incineration
(Controlled High-Temp Burning)		 • Target: Biomedical / Hospital waste (syringes, bandages) & toxic industrial waste.
• Burned in closed furnaces at $800^\circ\text{C}$ to $1,200^\circ\text{C}$.		 • Reduces weight and volume of waste by up to 90%.
• Completely destroys infectious pathogens/germs in medical waste.
• Heat generated can be used to produce steam and electricity (Waste-to-Energy).		 • Expensive to install and operate.
• Releases toxic gases (dioxins, furans, $SO_2$) and heavy-metal fly ash if chimneys lack scrubbers and electrostatic precipitators.
5. The 3Rs Hierarchy
(Reduce → Reuse → Recycle)		 • Reduce: Generate less waste at source (buy unpacked/durable goods, avoid single-use plastic).
• Reuse: Use items multiple times without reprocessing (glass jars, cloth bags, old clothes).
• Recycle: Reprocess waste into new raw material (paper, glass, metal, plastic).		 • Conserves scarce natural resources (trees, minerals, fossil fuels, water).
• Saves massive energy (e.g., recycling aluminium saves ~95% energy; recycling paper saves trees).
• Achieves Zero-Waste circular economy.		 • Reuse is better than Recycle because Reuse requires zero energy or industrial reprocessing.
• Plastics can only be down-cycled a limited number of times.
Term / Phenomenon Pollutant / Source Official CISCE Board Definition & Impact Keyword
1. Eutrophication
(Algal Bloom)		 Agricultural runoff (Nitrogen & Phosphorus fertilizers) + Domestic sewage/detergents entering lakes/ponds. Excess nutrients cause explosive growth of surface algae (Algal Bloom). This blocks sunlight and depletes Dissolved Oxygen (DO) (increases BOD), causing suffocation and mass death of fish and aquatic life.
2. Biomagnification
(Bioaccumulation)		 Non-biodegradable toxic chemicals: DDT, Mercury, Lead, Arsenic, PCBs in water/soil food chains. The progressive increase in the concentration of toxic non-biodegradable substances at each successive trophic level of a food chain (e.g., Water → Plankton → Small Fish → Large Fish → Fish-Eating Birds/Humans).
3. Leachate Rainwater percolating through open garbage dumps / unlined landfills. Foul, dark toxic liquid containing dissolved heavy metals and pathogens that seeps underground and contaminates groundwater aquifers and wells.
4. Minamata Disease & Itai-Itai • Mercury (Methylmercury): Minamata Disease.
• Cadmium: Itai-Itai ("Ouch-Ouch") disease.
• Nitrates: Blue Baby Syndrome.		 Minamata: Industrial mercury waste dumped into Minamata Bay (Japan) bioaccumulated in fish; humans eating the fish suffered fatal nerve/brain damage, paralysis, and blindness.
5. Acid Rain Sulphur Dioxide ($SO_2$) & Oxides of Nitrogen ($NO_x$) from thermal power plants, factories, and vehicles. Reacts with rainwater to form Sulphuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$) and Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$). Corrodes marble monuments (‘Marble Cancer’ on Taj Mahal), makes soil acidic, and kills fish.
6. Global Warming & Landfill Methane Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$), Methane ($CH_4$), Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$), CFCs. Anaerobic decomposition of organic waste in dumps releases Methane ($CH_4$ — Marsh Gas), a potent greenhouse gas that traps terrestrial heat, melting polar ice caps and raising sea levels.
7. Ozone Layer Depletion Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) from old refrigerators, ACs, aerosol sprays, and styrofoam. Chlorine atoms break down stratospheric Ozone ($O_3$), allowing harmful Ultraviolet (UV) rays to reach Earth, causing skin cancer, cataracts, lowered immunity, and damage to phytoplankton.
8. Bagasse
(Agro-Industrial Waste)		 Fibrous crushed sugarcane residue left after extracting juice in sugar mills. Used as raw material for manufacturing eco-friendly paper, paperboard, cardboard, and particle board (saving forest trees), and as biofuel for cogeneration of electricity.
9. Fly Ash
(Thermal Power Plant Waste)		 Fine powdery ash produced by burning pulverized coal in thermal power stations. Recycled to manufacture Fly Ash Bricks, Portland Pozzolana Cement, concrete blocks, and road embankments (prevents air pollution and saves topsoil used in clay bricks).
10. Waste Plastic in Road Construction Shredded non-biodegradable waste plastic (carry bags, wrappers, PET bottles). Mixed with heated Bitumen (asphalt) to lay durable, water-resistant, pothole-free ‘Plastic Roads’ (pioneered in India by Prof. Rajagopalan Vasudevan).
