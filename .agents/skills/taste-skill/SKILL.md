# Taste Skill v2 (Anti-Slop Art Direction)

A page built by an AI model usually looks like the average of every page the model has seen: a centered headline over three lines, a small uppercase label above it, a row of three identical rounded cards, a soft purple gradient, and a monospace tag. Nothing in it is broken, but nothing in it was intentionally decided.

Taste Skill replaces those defaults with bold, deliberate design decisions.

---

## The Four Anti-Slop Principles

### 1. The Concept Comes from the Subject Matter
What separates award-winning sites from forgettable templates is a visual concept taken from the real subject itself, not from generic internet tech tropes.
- **Identify the Subject's Real Artifacts**: In education/examination platforms, this means authentic board marking schemes, timed countdown pressure, structured rubric matrices, specimen papers, and clean distraction-free study vaults.
- **Ban Template Chrome**: Eliminate random floating badges, fake star ratings, decorative waves that mean nothing, and cookie-cutter 3-card rows.

### 2. Substance Over Superficial Restraint
Taking away generic ornaments is only half the job. A page that loses its icons and cards without gaining bold character reads as empty and thin.
The other half is **substance**:
- Type set larger and with deliberate presence.
- Broad fields of intentional background color with high contrast.
- Every section carrying deep, real information (not dummy placeholder text).

### 3. Deliberate Spatial Tension & Asymmetry
- **Asymmetric Layouts**: Break the monotony of centered stacks. Place headlines flush left with wide right margins, or juxtapose compact data tables next to dominant focus headlines.
- **Varied Cadence**: Alternate between dense, high-information zones (e.g., question clusters, formula tables) and spacious, breathable breathing zones.

### 4. Typography that Carries Personality
- Never use unopinionated generic system font defaults.
- Pair a distinctive, personality-rich display font (e.g., `Outfit`, `Cabinet Grotesk`, `Playfair Display`, `Syne`, or `Space Grotesk`) with an ultra-clean, legible body font (`Inter`, `Plus Jakarta Sans`, or `Geist`).
- Treat headline typography as an active visual architecture element, not just neutral text.

---

## Rules of the House Style (Banned AI Tells)

1. **NO "AI-Purple / Indigo" Washes**: Never use the default purple-to-blue linear gradients on buttons or backgrounds unless explicitly commanded. Use authentic palettes: deep charcoal, obsidian, warm paper, cream, terracotta, slate, midnight navy, or vivid amber.
2. **NO Redundant All-Caps Eyebrow Tags**: Do not put `// CHAPTER 01` or `EXPLORE FEATURES` above every heading. Headings should be clear enough on their own.
3. **NO Cookie-Cutter SaaS Cards**: Stop dividing every piece of content into identical rounded rectangular cards with the exact same border-radius and grey shadow. Use structural divider rules, tables, bento scaling, or seamless rows.
4. **NO Emoji Used as Interface Icons**: Use clean inline SVGs or geometric accents rather than emoji for system navigation.
5. **NO Trailing "→" on Every Button**: Avoid sticking arrows onto every action button. Write clear, action-oriented verbs ("Launch Test", "Open DPP Vault", "View Analysis").

## The Look-and-Fix Verification Loop
After implementing any interface:
1. **Squint Test**: Does the page have a clear visual hierarchy? What is the single dominant element your eye hits first?
2. **Density Balance**: Is the text cramped or floating in a void? Adjust margins so that related elements feel bound together and unrelated elements have clear separation.
3. **Interactive Feedback**: Do cards lift or outline smoothly on hover? Do buttons give immediate, tactile feedback on click?
